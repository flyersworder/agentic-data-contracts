Context-layer evaluations compare an analytics agent with and without curated documentation, which confounds what the layer documents with how it delivers it. We vary one with the other held fixed, using a data contract that can be emptied, given extra facts in identical words, pasted into the prompt or looked up. On DABStep, emptying the contract's knowledge while keeping its tools costs 26 to 50 hard-task points on six models. On the three models run three times, its lead over the pasted manual, 20 to 43 hard-task points, is mostly two pieces of data semantics the manual omits: an empty list that means "all", and fees that add up when several rules match. Stated in prose, they recover most of the lead on GPT-6 luna and, end-to-end, all of it on Qwen 3.8; the second lifts accuracy by 19 and 34 points on tasks outside the transcripts read to find it. With the facts held equal, the contract's representation still leads on the rule-matching tasks (+3.7 and +5.5 points, provisional). Looking the contract up shows no advantage end-to-end once both arms state the two facts, though on Qwen's official scoring it leads; on LiveSQLBench the route makes no detectable difference. Lookup also works where pasting the contract's YAML would not fit, and logs what each answer consulted.
