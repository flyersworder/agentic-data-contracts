Context-layer evaluations compare an analytics agent with and without
curated documentation, which confounds what the layer documents with how it
delivers it. We vary one with the other held fixed, using a data contract
that can be emptied, given extra facts in identical words, pasted into the
prompt or looked up. On DABStep, emptying the contract's knowledge while
keeping its tools costs 26 to 50 hard-task points on six models. On the
three models run three times, its lead over the pasted manual, 20 to 43
hard-task points, is mostly two pieces of
data semantics the manual omits: an empty list that means ``all'', and fees
that add up when several rules match. Stated in prose, they recover most of
the lead on GPT-6 luna and, end-to-end, all of it on Qwen~3.8; the second
lifts accuracy by 19 and 34 points on tasks outside the transcripts read
to find it. With the facts held equal and stated once, the contract's
representation still leads on the rule-matching tasks (+6.5 and +4.9
points, unregistered). Written into the contract, the two facts are
delivered no better by lookup than by pasting the contract, though on
Qwen's official scoring lookup leads; on LiveSQLBench the route makes no
detectable difference. Lookup also works where pasting
the contract's YAML would not fit, and logs what each answer consulted.
