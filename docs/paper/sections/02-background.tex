\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Two benchmarks with documentation}
\label{sec:dabstep}

DABStep~\cite{dabstep}, built by Adyen and Hugging Face, asks
data-analysis questions over a payments warehouse (138{,}236
transactions, a fee-rule table, merchant reference data) with two
human-written documents, \texttt{manual.md} and a dataset README. Its
difficulty is semantic rather than syntactic: the hard split turns on the
fee-matching rule, in which a missing value in a rule means \emph{applies
to every value}, and an agent that reads only the schema returns a clean,
wrong number. Its golds are withheld and grading is centralised, which
raises the cost of the adaptive overfitting a public test set
invites~\cite{ladder,leaderboard-illusion}. Its question space is small:
by our grouping of its templates, 450 test tasks expand 113 question shapes, 12 of which cover half
the benchmark, and 294 of our 332 hard tasks concern fees. The benchmark's
paper reports its strongest agent at 14.55\% on the hard split; credible
named leaderboard entries since cluster around 20 to
26\%~\cite{dabstep-leaderboard}.

LiveSQLBench~\cite{livesqlbench} supplies, for each of its 22 databases, a
knowledge base of metric, class and convention definitions written for the
benchmark, so its documentation is native rather than a vendor manual
repurposed. Its knowledge is spread across many entries, most of them used by a
single task, rather than concentrated in one procedure. BIRD-Interact builds on
LiveSQLBench by masking knowledge on purpose~\cite{bird-interact}; we ask
how much of it the released KB leaves open without masking. Enterprise
benchmarks whose schemas exceed a context window~\cite{spider2,beaver} are
outside our scope; of DataAgentBench, whose datasets span several database
systems~\cite{dab-berkeley}, we load nine into DuckDB for one registered
test (Section~\ref{sec:threats}).

\subsection{Three kinds of layer}
\label{sec:layers}

Three families of approach are routinely conflated. \emph{Semantic layers}
(MetricFlow~\cite{metricflow}, Cube~\cite{cube},
LookML~\cite{lookml}; for agents, Cortex Analyst's semantic
models~\cite{cortex-analyst} and Genie~\cite{genie}) define metrics as
executable artifacts, so the agent calls a metric rather than deriving it;
their database precedents are answering queries using
views~\cite{halevy-views} and ontology-based data access, which maps a
domain vocabulary onto a schema~\cite{obda-survey}. \emph{Context layers} store
natural-language documentation next to the data and retrieve it at query
time; MotherDuck Guides~\cite{motherduck-guides} is the best-documented
instance. \emph{Data contracts} come from governance: a declarative
artifact stating what a dataset contains and what may be done with it,
with the Open Data Contract Standard~\cite{odcs} the emerging
specification\ifextended, in a line of dataset documentation from
datasheets~\cite{datasheets} to Croissant~\cite{croissant}\fi. The artifact under test is of the third kind, and closest to a semantic
model with an access policy: one YAML supplies metrics as SQL with their
descriptions, which the agent reads and composes rather than calls, and
the rules its tools enforce.

\subsection{What has been measured}
\label{sec:prior}

\textbf{Context layers on DABStep.} MotherDuck's Guides raise DABStep
accuracy by 72 points and cut cost by 55\%~\cite{motherduck-guides}; a
second report finds vector-search retrieval ``capped out around 88\%'' and
a hierarchical semantic layer, refined iteratively, reaching
100\%~\cite{motherduck-semantic}. The system atop the validated leaderboard
validates helper functions against the benchmark's
golds~\cite{nvidia-kgmon}, and others encode the fee procedure as
versioned code~\cite{oceanbase-datapilot}. \ifextended Pre-built views suit a question
space enumerable in advance, which is DABStep's construction; we measure
the layer that must cover the unanticipated question. \fi Tuned layers also
do not travel~\cite{evoontology}.

\textbf{Documentation as knowledge.} BIRD withholds and supplies
human-written evidence, worth 20 points to GPT-4~\cite{bird}; KaggleDBQA
finds database documentation doubles parser accuracy~\cite{kaggledbqa};
an ontology over an enterprise schema moves GPT-4 from 16\% to
54\%~\cite{sequeda-kg}; a semantic-layer benchmark compares a curated metric
layer with text-to-SQL~\cite{dbt-benchmark}, and another a schema with and
without a semantic document~\cite{semantic-paired}. Text-to-SQL pipelines
add decomposition, schema linking and self-correction around the
model~\cite{din-sql,mac-sql,chess,dail-sql}; we hold the agent loop fixed
and vary what it is told. Evidence can
also be generated, by a model trained for it or from the
data~\cite{knowledge-to-sql,seed}, the precedent for our compiled and
authored contracts. These measure the value of the content. None removes it
while holding its delivery fixed, or holds it equal while varying the
delivery. Benchmark golds are often wrong or
ambiguous~\cite{annotation-errors,bird-noise,ambrosia}; our determinacy
ratings (Section~\ref{sec:lsb-knowledge}) measure a related property, how
much of the gold the documentation settles.

\textbf{Delivery: long context and retrieval.} The same material can be
put whole in the prompt or retrieved when needed. For NL2SQL, long-context
models given the full schema, documentation, hints and examples prove
robust to the extra material~\cite{chung-long-context}, and schema linking stops paying
once the schema fits~\cite{death-schema-linking}; in general, long context
matches or beats retrieval in accuracy while retrieval is far
cheaper~\cite{rag-or-long-context}, retrieval helps long-context models
too~\cite{retrieval-meets-long-context}, loading
tool descriptions on demand cuts prompt size~\cite{rag-mcp}, and
practitioner guidance favours loading context just in time over preloading
it~\cite{anthropic-context}. Long prompts
do lose accuracy where the needle shares no words with the question, where
distractors resemble it, as tasks grow harder~\cite{nolima,context-rot,ruler},
and even from length alone with retrieval
perfect~\cite{length-alone-hurts};
retrieval quality also falls for material in the middle of a long
prompt~\cite{lost-in-the-middle}. Section~\ref{sec:scale} measures delivery
for definitions an agent writes SQL from, in the easy regime of that
literature, and confirms its accuracy and cost results there. Retrieving
documentation next to where code is written helps code
generation~\cite{docprompting}, and a prompt's representation of the same
schema moves text-to-SQL accuracy~\cite{dail-sql}, our ``form''. A tool's
documentation shapes what an agent does with it~\cite{tool-documentation};
emptying a component's content while holding its form fixed, our hollow
control, is the move of~\cite{min-demonstrations}, applied there to
demonstrations.

\textbf{Governance.} Capabilities can be enforced outside the model, as
in CaMeL~\cite{camel}; agents' compliance with a policy given in the
prompt is what $\tau$-bench measures~\cite{tau-bench}; and models break
simple stated rules with no adversary present~\cite{rules}, the
expectation Section~\ref{sec:governance} runs against. Least privilege, a
read-only role, removes the hazard outright and is the baseline any
deployment should keep.

What is new here is a protocol that holds knowledge equal where it
matters: a control that removes the content while holding the scaffolding
fixed, notes that add one fact at a time in identical words, and the same
knowledge by two routes, with 76 predictions committed in advance. Its
finding is specific: an apparent delivery advantage on DABStep is mostly
two pieces of data semantics one side omits. We do not rank contracts
against documentation retrieval, which no arm runs; we attribute what a
layer's content does and compare routes for the same content.
