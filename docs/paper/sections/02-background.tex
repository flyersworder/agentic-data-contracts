\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Two benchmarks with documentation}
\label{sec:dabstep}

DABStep~\cite{dabstep}, built by Adyen and Hugging Face, asks
data-analysis questions over a payments warehouse (138{,}236
transactions, a fee-rule table, merchant reference data) with two
human-written documents, \texttt{manual.md} and a dataset README. Its
difficulty is semantic rather than syntactic: the hard split turns on the
fee-matching rule, in which a missing value in a rule means \emph{applies
to every value}, and an agent that reads only the schema returns a clean,
wrong number. Its golds are withheld and grading is centralised, which
raises the cost of the overfitting that makes many text-to-SQL results
hard to trust~\cite{ladder,leaderboard-illusion}. Its question space is
small: 450 test tasks expand 113 question shapes, 12 of which cover half
the benchmark, and 294 of our 332 hard tasks concern fees. The benchmark's
paper reports its strongest agent at 14.55\% on the hard split; credible
named leaderboard entries since cluster around 20 to 26\%.

LiveSQLBench~\cite{livesqlbench} supplies, for each of its 22 databases, a
knowledge base of metric, class and convention definitions written for the
benchmark, so its documentation is native rather than a vendor manual
repurposed. Its knowledge is spread across many entries, most of them used by a
single task, rather than concentrated in one procedure. The two benchmarks
therefore differ in exactly the property this paper turns out to need:
whether the documentation determines what the gold requires.

\subsection{Three kinds of layer}
\label{sec:layers}

Three families of approach are routinely conflated. \emph{Semantic layers}
(dbt metrics, Cube, LookML) define metrics as executable artifacts, so the
agent calls a metric rather than deriving it. \emph{Context layers} store
natural-language documentation next to the data and retrieve it at query
time; MotherDuck Guides~\cite{motherduck-guides} is the best-documented
instance. \emph{Data contracts} come from governance: a declarative
artifact stating what a dataset contains and what may be done with it,
with the Open Data Contract Standard~\cite{odcs} the emerging
specification, in a line of dataset documentation from
datasheets~\cite{datasheets} to Croissant~\cite{croissant}. The artifact
under test is of the third kind and serves two purposes at once: the same
YAML supplies the semantics the agent reads and the rules the tool layer
enforces. A view or macro is compiled ahead of time by a human and covers
the metrics somebody anticipated; a contract is compiled per query by the
model and covers whatever question arrives.

\subsection{What has been measured}
\label{sec:prior}

\textbf{Context layers on DABStep.} MotherDuck's Guides raise DABStep
accuracy by 72 points and cut cost by 55\%~\cite{motherduck-guides}. A
second report gives a progression rather than a number: retrieving
context fragments by vector search ``capped out around 88\%'', baking the
knowledge into the warehouse as comments, macros and derived tables
reached 93\%, and a hierarchical semantic layer refined iteratively reached
100\%~\cite{motherduck-semantic}. The system atop the validated leaderboard,
at 89.95\% hard, validates candidate helper functions against the
benchmark's gold answers~\cite{nvidia-kgmon}; nothing forbids that, and we
allege no violation. Pre-built views are most effective when the question
space is enumerable in advance, which is DABStep's construction; our
figures measure the layer that must cover the unanticipated question, so
they measure a harder task, not a worse attempt at the same one. The tuned
artifacts also do not travel: the author of the 100\% result calls it
``coupled'' to the serving model~\cite{motherduck-semantic}, and
EvoOntology measures that coupling, losing 6.6 to 10.9 points when its
evolved layer serves a different model~\cite{evoontology}.

\textbf{Documentation as knowledge.} BIRD withholds and supplies
human-written evidence, worth 20 points to GPT-4~\cite{bird}; KaggleDBQA
finds database documentation doubles parser accuracy~\cite{kaggledbqa};
an ontology over an enterprise schema moves GPT-4 from 16\% to
54\%~\cite{sequeda-kg}. These isolate the value of the content. None
separates it from the scaffolding that delivers it, asks whether the
documentation determines the answer, or asks who wrote it.

\textbf{Delivery: long context and loading on demand.} Retrieval quality
falls for material placed in the middle of a long
prompt~\cite{lost-in-the-middle}, and the belief that a model loses
relevant context as a prompt grows is one reason agent frameworks load
documentation, tools and skills on demand instead of up front. That
pattern is usually argued for rather than measured. Section~\ref{sec:scale}
measures it on contracts of up to 551k tokens. A tool's documentation
shapes what an agent does with it~\cite{tool-documentation}; emptying a
component's content while holding its form fixed, our hollow control, is
the move of~\cite{min-demonstrations}, applied there to demonstrations.

\textbf{Governance.} Capabilities can be enforced outside the model, as
in CaMeL~\cite{camel}; agents' compliance with a policy given in the
prompt is what $\tau$-bench measures~\cite{tau-bench}; and models break
simple stated rules with no adversary present~\cite{rules}, the
expectation Section~\ref{sec:governance} runs against.

What is new here is the decomposition on two benchmarks, with knowledge
held equal by construction where it matters: a control that removes the
content while holding the scaffolding fixed, notes that add one fact at a
time, the same knowledge delivered three ways, three ways of authoring it,
and 53 predictions fixed before their runs.
