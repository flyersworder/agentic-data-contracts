The four-arm ablation separates the contract's knowledge from the
scaffolding that delivers it. \armH{} keeps the contract's tools, its
instruction to look things up before writing SQL, its table allow-list,
its operation rules and every name and type, and empties every description
and SQL definition (Section~\ref{sec:hollow}). It therefore measures the
knowledge given that scaffolding, not all knowledge against none.
Table~\ref{tab:decomposition} gives the hard-task accuracy of all four arms
on six models: two panels repeated three times, and four single runs.
(\mLuna{} did not run the hollow arm.)

\textbf{The knowledge step is large on all six models, and robust to
clustering.} Restoring the knowledge (\armH{} to \armC{}) adds 50.0
hard-task points on \mSol{} (205 tasks to 12 over the three repeats; 95\%
interval over the 38 question shapes $+32.1$ to $+66.2$, 23 shapes to 5)
and 34.5 to 43.8 on \mQwen{} depending on the scoring view (189 tasks to
10 and to 8; over shapes, from $+24$ at the lowest). On the four single
runs it adds 26.2 to 35.8 points. Nearly all of it is the fee procedure:
on the 294 hard tasks that concern fees the step is $+57.6$ on \mSol{}; on
the other 38 it is $-8.8$ on \mSol{} and $-0.9$ on \mQwen{} (end-to-end).

\textbf{The empty scaffolding is worth $-9$ to $+15$ points, and at most
$+0.2$ on the repeated panels.} In the single runs the step is $+0.0$ on
\mDS{}, $+5.4$ on \mGLM{} and $+14$ to $+15$ on \mSON{} and \mGPT{}. On
\mSol{} the empty tools cost 8.5 hard-task points (23 tasks to 66; over
shapes $-16$ to $-2$); on \mQwen{} the step is $-5.5$ under the official
verdicts and $+0.2$ end-to-end. Empty tools can mislead: the agent calls
\texttt{lookup\_domain}, is told nothing is available, and searches harder
(\appref{app:efficiency}). On every one of the six models the knowledge
step exceeds the scaffolding step. A contract that helped by narrowing the
tables, imposing a procedure or slowing the agent down would show the
opposite.

\subsection{The contract's lead over the manual}
\label{sec:panel-gaps}

The manual pasted into the prompt carries the same domain, and it helps
too: \armM{} beats \armS{} by 21.6 hard-task points on \mSol{} and by 6.3
(official) or 20.8 (end-to-end) on \mQwen{}. The contract leads the manual
by 19.9 hard-task points on \mSol{} (116 tasks to 9; over shapes $+7$ to
$+34$) and by 22.7 (official) or 23.2 (end-to-end) on \mQwen{}, on the two
repeated panels. On \mLuna{}, in its own run, it leads by 42.9 (79.6\%
against 36.7\%). Both arms hold one domain, so this looks like a delivery
advantage: the rule one lookup away, against the rule somewhere in a long
prompt. The rest of this section shows that on \mLuna{} and \mQwen{} it is
mostly two pieces of data semantics the manual omits.

\subsection{The first semantic: a wildcard stored as an empty list}
\label{sec:one-fact}
\label{sec:interpretation}

The manual says a null fee field ``applies to all possible values''. The
fee data never uses null for its three list-typed fields; it stores ``all''
as an empty list, on 720 of 1{,}000 rules for \texttt{account\_type}. The
contract's author took that from the data and built it into the matching
predicate. \armMw{} is \armM{} plus that one fact, stated once in words and
without SQL; its plan and the reading of each outcome were committed before
its first run. Over all 401 tasks it recovers 77\% of the gap between
manual and contract on \mSol{} (\armM{} 65.1\%, \armMw{} 77.3\%, \armC{}
81.0\%; 90 tasks to 28 over \armM{}; over shapes 0.16 to 1.01), 41\% on
\mQwen{} and 38\% on \mLuna{}. The shares' intervals overlap widely, so
their order across models is not established.

The fact acts where it should. On \mSol{} it recovers 112\% of the gap on
the 202 tasks outside rule matching, and 8\% on the 199 rule-matching
tasks, those that match each payment against the table of fee rules
(68.5\% to 69.3\%, against the contract's 79.4\%). On the three families
that need only the right set of fee rules it reaches the contract (180 of
180 task-runs, against 42 for \armM{}); on the three total-fee families it
does nothing.

\subsection{The second semantic: matching fees add up}
\label{sec:second-fact}

On \mLuna{}'s rule-matching tasks, \armMw{} still trailed the contract by
29 points. We read the transcripts of the 34 tasks the contract's text
solved and \armMw{} missed in both of the first two repeats. All 34 add up
the fees of many transactions, and about 30 turn on what happens when
several fee rules match one transaction, which the manual never says. The
contract's total-fee definition sums over every matched pair, the gold's
convention, without marking it as a choice
(Section~\ref{sec:authoring-paths}). The prose arm saw the overlap and
invented a one-rule policy; twice it computed the gold and rejected it as
double counting.

\armMws{} adds that convention to \armMw{} as a second note. On \mQwen{},
none of whose transcripts was read, it lifts the prose on the
rule-matching tasks by 42.7 points (112 tasks to 4), as registered in
(28Q). On \mLuna{} the registered lift (28) is 28.5 points (95 tasks to
4). Because the 34 inspected tasks were chosen from \mLuna{}'s runs, the
lift is also measured on the 165 rule-matching tasks outside that set:
$+19.2$ points on \mLuna{} (64 tasks to 4) and $+33.9$ on \mQwen{} (78 to
4), with shape intervals $+8.5$ to $+31.9$ and $+17.7$ to $+53.6$, under
every rater family. Excluding the 34 removes tasks the comparator failed
twice, so the selection biases this lift down; the third repeat alone,
which played no part in choosing them, gives $+20.6$ on the 165.

\textbf{Stated in prose, the two semantics recover most of the contract's
lead on \mLuna{}, and on \mQwen{} all of it end-to-end.} On \mLuna{}'s
rule-matching tasks \armMws{} reaches 84.1\% against the contract's
82.6\% (separate runs). On \mQwen{}, in one run, the prose with both
facts \emph{outscores} the governed contract end-to-end: 86.1\% against
79.6\% on the rule-matching tasks and 80.0\% against 67.7\% over all 401.
On the official view the contract keeps a lead on the rule-matching tasks,
51.6\% against 45.6\%, and trails over all 401, 51.0\% against 54.4\%.
\mSol{} was given only the first fact, so its rule-matching gap, where the
second acts, is untested.

\subsection{How plainly the semantics are stated}
\label{sec:plain}

On \mQwen{} the prose with both notes beats the contract's own text by
16.6 points on the rule-matching tasks end-to-end (69 tasks to 14,
$p{=}7{\times}10^{-10}$; registered tie (29Q), failed), and the gap survives
clustering and every rater family. The contract's text carries both
semantics, but less plainly: it speaks of each transaction ``joined to
\emph{the} fee rule'' before saying to sum, and its empty-list clause
comes 28{,}000 characters after a matching rule that mentions null only.
\mQwen{} often took ``the fee rule'' at its word: an approximate keyword
count finds single-rule reasoning in 103 of its 199 rule-matching runs on
the contract's text, against 37 on \mLuna{}. Adding the two notes to the
contract's text lifts it by 18.9 points end-to-end (6.2 official; (31Q)).
\textbf{Whether a semantic is stated is not enough; how plainly it is
stated moves accuracy by as much as the semantic itself.}

\subsection{What a pre-computed layer would add}
\label{sec:ceiling}

\ifextended
Compiling the DABStep contract's own SQL expressions into two views gives
an oracle for the layer a context-layer product would ship; no agent is
given the views. Instantiated by us with each task's parameters, they
reproduce the gold answer on every one of the 176 hard tasks whose
question shape they cover, selected by a pattern over the question text.
On those tasks whatever an agent misses is a failure to derive from
knowledge it provably had. In the four single runs that \emph{derivation
gap} is 39.2 points on \mGLM{}, 37.3 on \mDS{}, 26.1 on \mSON{} and 5.1 on
\mGPT{}, falling with capability (single runs, so a trend, not a
per-model estimate), while
the shortfall on the 148 tasks that need reasoning beyond the views moves
under no ordering (\appref{app:buckets}). This is a coverage diagnostic,
not a comparison with executable semantic layers. The contract's gain also
stays inside its domain: on the hard tasks that do not concern fees it
holds no detectable advantage in any of the four runs
(\appref{app:domain}).
