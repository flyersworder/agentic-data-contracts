\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Run B lost 29\% of its rows}
\label{sec:truncation}

We state this before the numbers, because every run~B figure is conditioned on
it.

Roughly 1{,}000 rows into run~B, the pinned provider endpoint began returning
HTTP 429. The harness had no retry for that status, so each throttled request
was written to the results file as a terminal error: 466 of 468 error rows are
rate limits. Error rate was 0.2\% over the first 1{,}000 rows, 31\% over the
next 200, and 100\% thereafter. The sweep then \emph{finished early}, because a
throttled request fails instantly, and exited cleanly.

The loss costs power, not validity, and the evidence for that is specific:

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep2pt
\item Errors are near-uniform across arms --- 113, 116, 118 and 121 of 401
      rows. No arm is preferentially damaged.
\item 114 of the 122 lost tasks lost \emph{all four} arms together, because
      the runner schedules a task's arms close in time. Independent per-arm
      failure would have left roughly 99 complete tasks; 279 survived.
      Missingness is a property of \emph{when} a task ran.
\item Complete-case figures and per-arm figures over each arm's own
      denominator agree to within one point.
\end{itemize}

We therefore report run~B on the 279 tasks scoreable in all four arms. One
residual caveat: the lost tasks are slightly enriched for hard (87\% against
83\% overall), so run~B's absolute accuracies are marginally optimistic. Every
arm is affected identically.

\subsection{The ablation: content, not scaffolding}
\label{sec:ablation}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig-ablation.pdf}
\caption{The two steps of the ablation, on both models. Adding nine governed
tools, the retrieval instruction, the table allow-list and the operation rules
--- with the prose emptied --- moves hard accuracy by $+0.0$ to $+5.4$ points.
Restoring the prose moves it by $+34.1$ to $+35.8$. Bars are Wilson 95\%
intervals; the first step's intervals overlap on both models.}
\label{fig:ablation}
\end{figure}

On hard tasks the gain splits into a scaffolding step and a content step
(Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}):

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
 & \tabhead{Run A} & \tabhead{Run B} \\
\midrule
\armS{}                     & 13.9\% & 22.6\% \\
${+}$ tools, procedure (\armH{}) & 19.3\% & 22.6\% \\
${+}$ prose (\armC{})            & \textbf{55.1\%} & \textbf{56.6\%} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{The content step is the whole effect. The scaffolding step is not
distinguishable from zero.}

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
& $\Delta$ hard & discordant & $p$ \\
\midrule
Run A    & $+5.4$ pp & 55 (20/35) & 0.058 \\
Run B    & $+0.0$ pp & 34 (19/15) & 0.61 \\
Pooled   & ---       & 89 (39/50) & 0.29 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The two runs do not agree on the sign. Nine governed tools, the retrieval
instruction, the table allow-list and the operation rules --- with no semantics
behind them --- buy nothing measurable over a bare schema.

This is a null result and we hold it to the standard of one: it is compatible
with a small positive effect our sample cannot resolve. What it excludes is an
effect large enough to account for a meaningful share of the 35-point content
step.

The direction of the claim is worth stating plainly. A reader's first
objection to \armC{} is that the contract might be helping through some
mechanism other than its knowledge. \armH{} holds every such mechanism fixed
and lands on the bare-schema baseline. The null strengthens the paper rather
than qualifying it.

\paragraph{Empty tools are not free.} In run~A, \armH{} is the most expensive
arm per correct answer and burns the most reasoning of any arm (543{,}565
tokens against \armC{}'s 154{,}247). In run~B it makes the most tool calls of
any arm (6{,}375 against 4{,}155) for the fewest correct answers. The
transcripts show why: the agent calls \texttt{lookup\_domain}, is told nothing
is available, and searches harder.

\subsection{Capability interaction}
\label{sec:interaction}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig-interaction.pdf}
\caption{Hard accuracy against base-model capability, where capability is the
bare-schema arm's own accuracy. \armM{} climbs 20.0 points between the two
models while \armC{} moves 1.5, so the lines converge. \textbf{Two points per
line cannot distinguish convergence from a shared ceiling}
(Section~\ref{sec:interaction}); a third model is required and is not yet run.}
\label{fig:interaction}
\end{figure}

The two runs bracket an interaction, ordered by what each model achieves from a
bare schema (Figure~\ref{fig:interaction}):

\begin{table*}[t]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Hard tasks & \armS{} & \armM{} & \armC{} & \armC{}${-}$\armM{} \\
\midrule
\texttt{glm-5.3-flash}     & 13.9\% & 22.9\% & 55.1\% & $+32.2$ \\
\texttt{deepseek-v4-flash} & 22.6\% & 42.9\% & 56.6\% & $+13.7$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The margin over a hand-written prompt shrinks as the bare-schema
baseline rises.}
\label{tab:interaction}
\end{table*}

\armM{} improves by 20.0 points between the two models; \armC{} improves by
1.5. The contract arm lands in almost the same place on both while the
prompt-based arm climbs toward it.

Two readings, and our data do not separate them:

\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Structured context substitutes for capability.} The contract
      supplies what a weaker model cannot infer; a stronger model extracts more
      of the same semantics from prose unaided.
\item \textbf{The contract arm is near a benchmark ceiling.} Both models land
      at ${\approx}56\%$, and Section~\ref{sec:threats} identifies a 30-task
      template that no arm on either model solves.
\end{enumerate}

The near-identical 55.1\%/56.6\% is more consistent with a shared ceiling than
with coincidence, so reading~2 is not idle. Separating them requires a stronger
model, where reading~1 predicts continued convergence and reading~2 predicts
\armC{} pinned near 56\% while \armM{} rises to meet it. \textbf{We make no
claim about frontier models from these two runs.}

\subsection{Where the effect is absent}

\armM{} reproduces the published leaderboard band on run~A --- 22.9\% hard
against a 20--26\% band for credible named entrants, on a model weaker than any
of them. This is the check that licenses the comparison at all: the harness
measures what the leaderboard measures. On run~B the same arm reaches 42.9\%,
above the band, which is a statement about the model rather than the harness.

On easy tasks all four arms fall within overlapping intervals on both runs
(61--74\% and 70--91\%), and \armC{} is not the best of them --- \armM{} leads
on easy tasks in both runs. The contract does nothing for questions that need
no domain knowledge, which is what it should do.
