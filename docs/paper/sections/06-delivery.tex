\section{Delivery at Equal Knowledge}
\label{sec:delivery}

Section~\ref{sec:knowledge} held the delivery fixed and varied the
knowledge. This section holds the knowledge fixed and varies the delivery,
in two contrasts on \mLuna{} and \mQwen{}. \emph{Form} compares the
contract's text with prose, both carrying the two notes. \emph{Route}
compares the same contract looked up through the governed tools with the
same contract pasted into the prompt over plain tools. Each contrast is
read only between arms that ran together in the same repeats
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). \mSol{}'s knowledge-equal evidence is on the
constructed questions (Section~\ref{sec:gaps}) and the scale experiment
(Section~\ref{sec:scale}).

\subsection{Form: the contract's text against prose}
\label{sec:alike}

\armTws{} is the contract's text plus the same two notes as \armMws{},
word for word, so the two state the same facts and differ in form. The run
registered a tie, (30) and (30Q), and fixed in advance that a failure with
the contract's text ahead would read as the form adding to the facts.
\textbf{On the rule-matching tasks the contract's text leads}: by 3.7
points on \mLuna{} (32 tasks to 13; over question shapes $+0.6$ to $+7.9$,
robust to every rater family) and 5.5 on \mQwen{} (35 to 10; over shapes
$-0.4$ to $+10.4$, excluding zero only over fee families). On the other
202 tasks \mLuna{}'s lead is 9.6 points (51 tasks to 14), mostly in the
fee-average families, and \mQwen{}'s text trails by 5.3 (37 to 47, not
significant).

The rule-matching estimate is provisional, for five reasons. It is a
package of format, SQL definitions and repetition: \armTws{} states each
fact twice, once in the contract and once in the note. Neither model's
lead clears shape clustering on the 165 tasks outside the inspected set,
and on \mLuna{} it rests largely on the 34 inspected tasks. It is close to
the drift between two runs of one arm, $-2.8$ to $+1.7$ points across
eight reruns. And it is sensitive to the scorer's tolerance: requiring an
exact match to the gold, \mLuna{}'s lead falls to $+1.8$ (22 tasks to 11,
$p{=}0.08$; over shapes $-2.0$ to $+6.6$), while \mQwen{}'s keeps its
direction, $+9.7$ on the whole message and $+4.7$ on the recovered final
paragraph (both $p{<}0.05$). On the other 202 tasks \mLuna{}'s lead
survives exact matching ($+7.9$, 50 tasks to 18).

\armT{} and \armC{} hold one contract and differ in route: its files in
the prompt over plain tools, or the governed lookups, which also bring the
validator, the tool interface and the instruction to look things up
(Table~\ref{tab:route}). With the contract's original wording, the route
makes no detectable difference on \mLuna{}'s rule-matching tasks ($-0.7$,
15 tasks to 18) and adds 10.1 points on \mQwen{}'s, as registered in (27)
(64 tasks to 16; over shapes $+3$ to $+17$, though the shapes' own sign
test does not reject). On the other 202 tasks it gives $+5.1$ on \mLuna{}
and $-6.9$ on \mQwen{}, neither surviving clustering. In \mLuna{}'s
fee-average families the 50 runs the governed arm won all match what
dropping the wildcard rules gives; the governed arm got the rule from a
call just before its first fee query, and the validator played no part.

Experiment~5d asks whether the route still matters once both arms state
the two facts plainly. \armCws{} is the governed contract plus the two
notes, run together with \armC{} and \armTws{}. It tests governed delivery
of the rest of the contract with the deciding facts pasted in both arms,
not whether the facts reach the agent better by lookup. End-to-end on the
rule-matching tasks, \textbf{the route shows no advantage}: $-1.8$ on
\mLuna{} (16 tasks to 23, $p{=}0.34$; registered tie (41) holds) and
$-2.2$ on \mQwen{} (10 to 21, $p{=}0.071$). The latter fails the
registered tie (41Q), which required $p{\geq}0.10$, with the text ahead;
its shape interval is $-6.9$ to $+1.9$. Experiment~5's $+10.1$ does not
survive the plain wording end-to-end. On \mQwen{}'s official view,
however, the governed arm leads, 57.8\% against 52.4\% (75 tasks to 49,
$p{=}0.024$). The notes lift the governed contract on both models: $+4.0$
on \mLuna{} (22 tasks to 6; the registered (42) predicted no difference
and failed) and $+7.0$ on \mQwen{} ((42Q) holds).

\textbf{On \mQwen{}, the views split by which text is scored.} Of
\mQwen{}'s answers that are right end-to-end on the rule-matching tasks,
the governed arm gives the value alone, as the prompt asks, in 67\%,
against 59\% for the text (65\% against 60\% in experiment~5). Scoring the
whole message rewards that compliance; scoring the recovered final
paragraph does not. Part of every \mQwen{} route effect under the official
view is answer format, not a more often correct value (descriptive, not
predicted). \mLuna{} gives the value alone in every arm.

The other route contrasts point the same way. On the constructed
questions an authored contract by its two routes differs by $+0.5$ points
(2 tasks to 0), and the share of its gain that its prose notes reproduce
is 1.03 (Section~\ref{sec:gaps}). On LiveSQLBench, compiled knowledge in
the prompt or behind the tools scores 36.8\% against 35.9\% on \mLuna{}
(25 tasks to 22) and 37.7\% against 37.7\% on \mQwen{} (24 to 22);
registered prediction (3) had the tools ahead and failed.

\paragraph{Summary.} With the facts held equal, the contract's form leads
prose on the rule-matching tasks of both models, provisionally. The route
shows no advantage end-to-end once both arms state the facts plainly,
and on LiveSQLBench it makes no detectable difference; on \mQwen{}'s
official view it leads, largely through answer format. These contrasts
vary by model, task family and scoring view, and none is offered as a
bound on what delivery can do. How long a prompt the knowledge sits in is
a separate question (Section~\ref{sec:scale}).
