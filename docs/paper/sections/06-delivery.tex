\section{What Looked Like Delivery Was Knowledge}
\label{sec:delivery}

On the hard tasks the contract beats the manual by 20 to 23 points on the
repeated panels (Section~\ref{sec:decomposition}). Both carry the same
domain, so the obvious reading is delivery: the
contract's rule is one lookup away when the query is written, while the
manual's is buried in a long prompt. The experiments in this section
replaced one difference between the arms at a time with a controlled one,
in the order the transcripts suggested it, on \mSol{}, \mLuna{} and
\mQwen{}. Their arms are named in Table~\ref{tab:arms}.

\subsection{One fact the manual leaves out}
\label{sec:one-fact}
\label{sec:interpretation}

The manual says a null fee field ``applies to all possible values''. The
fee data never uses null for its three list-typed fields; it stores ``all''
as an empty list, on 720 of 1{,}000 rules for \texttt{account\_type}. The
contract's author took that from the data and built it into the matching
predicate. \armMw{} is \armM{} plus that one fact, stated once in words
and without SQL; its plan and the reading
of each outcome were committed before its first run. On \mSol{} it
recovers 77\% of the gap between manual and contract over all tasks
(\armM{} 65.1\%, \armMw{} 77.3\%, \armC{} 81.0\%; 90 tasks to 28 over
\armM{}), on \mQwen{} 41\%, and on \mLuna{} 38\%, as predicted for \mQwen{}
and contrary to the prediction for \mLuna{}; over question shapes the
three shares' intervals overlap widely (\mLuna{} 0.16 to 0.60), so the
ordering across models is not established. The fact acts where it should:
on the three families that need only the right set of fee rules, \armMw{}
reaches the contract on \mSol{} (180 of 180 task-runs against 42 for
\armM{}). On the three total-fee families it does nothing, and the
contract still wins there by 32 tasks to 0.

\subsection{A second fact, found in the transcripts}
\label{sec:second-fact}

On \mLuna{}'s 199 rule-matching tasks, those that match each payment
against the table of fee rules, \armMw{} still trailed the contract by 29
points. We read the transcripts of the 34 tasks the contract's text solved
and \armMw{} missed in both of the first two repeats. All 34 add up the
fees of many transactions, and about 30 turn on what happens when several
fee rules match one transaction, which the manual never says. The
contract's total-fee definition sums over every matched pair, the gold
convention, without marking that as an interpretation
(Section~\ref{sec:authoring-paths}). The prose arm saw the overlap and
invented a one-rule policy; twice it computed the gold number and rejected
it as double counting.

\armMws{} adds that convention to \armMw{} as a second note. On
\mQwen{}, none of whose transcripts was read, it lifts the prose on the
rule-matching tasks by 42.7 points (112 tasks to 4), as registered in
(28Q). On \mLuna{} the registered lift (28) is 28.5 points (95 tasks to
4), but that comparison reuses the runs that picked the 34 inspected
tasks. On the third repeat alone, which played no part in picking them, it
is 28.6 points, so the selection does not inflate it overall. On the 165
held-out tasks, whose transcripts nobody inspected, the lift is 19.2 points
on \mLuna{} (64 tasks to 4) and 33.9 on \mQwen{} (78 to 4); having dropped
tasks the manual failed twice, the held-out set if anything understates
it. Over question shapes both lifts stay far from zero (\mLuna{} $+14$ to
$+43$). \textbf{Most of what looked like delivery was these two
facts.} With both stated, the prose matches the contract's text on
\mLuna{}: the contract's text is $+0.8$ points ahead in the registered tie
test (29), which paired a separately run arm by task and repeat, and the
prose 3.7 points ahead when the two ran together (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}); over shapes the tie's interval,
$-4.5$ to $+5.8$, just exceeds the 5-point bound.

On \mQwen{}, the prose with both notes led the contract's text by 16.6
points in the second-fact run (end-to-end; 13.4 when they ran together,
Table~\ref{tab:ladder}), which no prediction foresaw. The two did not state
the facts alike. The
prose states each fact as a note of its own. The contract states the
summing convention less plainly: it speaks of each transaction ``joined to
\emph{the} fee rule'' before saying to sum. And its empty-list clause
comes 28{,}000 characters after a matching rule that mentions null only.
\mQwen{} often took ``the fee rule'' at its word: an approximate keyword
count finds single-rule reasoning in 103 of its 199 rule-matching runs on
the contract's text, against 37 on \mLuna{}.

\armTws{} is \armT{} plus the same two notes, word for word, and ran
together with \armMws{} and \armT{} (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). The run
registered a tie, (30) and (30Q), and fixed in advance how a failure with
the contract's text ahead would read: with the facts alike, the
contract's form adds to them. That is what happened. On the rule-matching
tasks the contract's text leads the prose by 3.7 points on \mLuna{} (32
tasks to 13; 95\% interval over question shapes $+0.6$ to $+7.9$, 11
shapes to 2) and 5.5 on \mQwen{} (35 to 10; $-0.4$ to $+10.4$ over shapes,
$+1.3$ to $+9.3$ over families). \textbf{With the facts equal, the contract's form
adds about 4 to 6 points on the rule-matching tasks,} with three
qualifications. Elsewhere the models part: on the other 202 tasks
\mLuna{}'s noted text leads the prose by 9.5 points (51 tasks to 14),
mostly in the fee-average families, and \mQwen{}'s trails it by 5.3 (37 to
47, not significant; \emph{expl.}). \armTws{} states each
fact twice, once in the contract and once in the note, so part of it may
be repetition. And it is about the size of the drift between two runs of
one arm: across eight such reruns the drift ranged from $-2.8$ to $+1.7$
points, none of it distinguishable from zero over shapes (\emph{expl.}). On the held-out tasks the lead is 4.6 points on
\mQwen{} and 2.2 on \mLuna{} (\emph{expl.}), not detectably different from
the inspected ones'.

\subsection{What the governed tools add}
\label{sec:tools-add}

\armT{} and \armC{} hold the same contract and differ only in delivery:
the files in the prompt over plain tools, or the governed lookups.
\textbf{Looking a rule up as the query is written helps on some fee
tasks, and which ones depends on the model.} On \mLuna{}'s
rule-matching tasks the two tie ($-0.7$ points, 15 tasks to 18). On its
other 202 tasks the governed arm leads by 5 points (38 tasks to 15,
$p{=}0.002$, \emph{expl.}), but 33 of the 38 come from three families that
average fees over the rule table, and over question shapes the interval,
$-1.8$ to $+11.1$, includes zero. In those families, 50 runs
went right for the governed arm and wrong for the text arm on the same
task and repeat. In every one, the text arm's answer equals, to six
decimals, what one gets by dropping the wildcard rules. The text arm had
the rule three times in its 60{,}000-character prompt. The governed arm
got it from a call made just before its first fee query: the matching
metric's lookup in 34 runs, \texttt{lookup\_domain} or the governed
\texttt{describe\_table} in 16. The validator played no part; none of the
50 had a rejection. On \mQwen{} the tools add 10.1 points on the
rule-matching tasks, as registered in (27) (64 tasks to 16; over shapes
$+3$ to $+17$), and trail the text on the rest by 6.9 (30 to 49, not
distinguishable from zero over shapes). Retrieval next to the point of use is the mechanism DocPrompting
exploits for code~\cite{docprompting}; here its effect depends on the
model and the family.

\subsection{Complete prose carries even a procedure}
\label{sec:procedures}

DABStep's contract gain sits on its rule-matching tasks. Two blind raters
scored the procedural load of the knowledge each question needs, on both
benchmarks shuffled together. On DABStep the gain sits on the high-load
tasks (119 tasks to 20, none on the rest), and within them on the top
level (116 to 14), where every task is the fee procedure. That left ``a
rule-matching procedure'' and ``this procedure'' confounded, so experiment
4 built new ones (Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks}): a rule-matching procedure
and a composite of dependent formulas for each of three databases, each
stated completely, once plainly and once in the style of a business manual
at twice the length. Every knowledge arm, prose or contract, in either
style, scores between 95.6\% and 100\% on each piece, on \mLuna{},
\mSol{}, and \mQwen{} (added after that ceiling was seen).
\textbf{Neither the type of knowledge nor the style of the prose decides
it}; on LiveSQLBench's own determined tasks the high-load ones gain
nothing from the contract either (8 tasks to 5, $p{=}0.58$).

\paragraph{Summary.} The contract's lead over the manual was mostly two
facts the manual did not state. With the facts worded alike, the
contract's form adds about 4 to 6 points on the rule-matching tasks, more
or less elsewhere depending on the model, and its lookups help on some fee
families. How
long a prompt the knowledge sits in is a separate question;
Section~\ref{sec:scale} finds no loss up to four times the contract's size,
in an easy regime.
