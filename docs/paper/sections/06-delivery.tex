\section{Delivery at Equal Knowledge}
\label{sec:delivery}

Section~\ref{sec:knowledge} held the delivery fixed and varied the
knowledge. This section holds the knowledge fixed and varies the delivery,
in two contrasts on \mLuna{} and \mQwen{}. \emph{Form} compares the
contract's text with prose stating the same two facts. \emph{Route}
compares the same contract looked up through the governed tools with the
same contract pasted into the prompt over plain tools. Each contrast is
read only between arms that ran together in the same repeats
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder}), with the shape-level engine of
Section~\ref{sec:prereg}.

\subsection{Form: the contract's text against prose}
\label{sec:alike}

\armTws{} is the contract's text plus the same two notes as \armMws{},
word for word. The run registered a tie, (30) and (30Q), and fixed in
advance that a failure with the contract's text ahead would read as the
form adding to the facts. \textbf{On the rule-matching tasks the
contract's text leads}: by 3.7 points on \mLuna{} (32 tasks to 13) and 5.5
on \mQwen{} (35 to 10). That estimate was provisional: \armTws{} states
each fact twice, in the contract and in the note; neither lead clears
shape clustering on the 165 tasks outside the inspected set; it is close
to the drift between two runs of one arm ($-2.8$ to $+1.7$ points across
eight reruns); and under exact matching \mLuna{}'s lead falls to $+1.8$
($p{=}0.08$).

Experiment~5e removes the repetition. \armTp{} pastes a contract with each
fact written once, in the notes' words, where it is used; beside
\armMws{}, run together, the two state the same facts in the same words
and differ in form. The contract's text again leads (expl.): $+6.5$ points
on \mLuna{} (90\% interval $+2.0$ to $+11.5$, shape $p{=}0.014$; under
exact matching $+4.4$, $p{=}0.012$) and $+4.9$ on \mQwen{} ($+0.9$ to
$+8.4$, $p{=}0.059$; $+2.2$ on its recovered final paragraph). It remains a
package of format and SQL definitions, and it was not registered.

\subsection{Route: looked up or pasted}
\label{sec:tools-add}

\armT{} and \armC{} hold one contract and differ in route: its files in
the prompt over plain tools, or the governed lookups, which also bring the
validator, the tool interface and the instruction to look things up.
With the contract's original wording, the route
makes no detectable difference on \mLuna{}'s rule-matching tasks ($-0.7$,
15 tasks to 18) and adds 10.1 points on \mQwen{}'s, as registered in (27)
(64 tasks to 16; shape $p{=}0.02$). Rerun in experiment~5e it gives $+1.5$
and $+7.0$ (shape $p{=}0.056$). In \mLuna{}'s fee-average families the 50
runs the governed arm won all match what dropping the wildcard rules gives;
the governed arm got the rule from a call just before its first fee query,
and the validator played no part.

With both facts as notes in both prompts (5d, \armCws{} against
\armTws{}), the governed route delivers the rest of the contract no
better: $-1.8$ points on \mLuna{} (tie (41) holds) and $-2.2$ on \mQwen{}
(tie (41Q) fails, text ahead); the notes lift the governed contract by 4.0
and 7.0 ((42) fails, (42Q) holds). Experiment~5e asks whether lookup
delivers the deciding facts themselves better. \armCp{} writes them into the contract
and states them in no prompt, so the agent reaches them only by lookup:
one \texttt{lookup\_domain("fees")} returns both. \textbf{The route does
not deliver the facts better}: against its paste \armTp{}, $-2.5$ points
on \mLuna{} (90\% interval $-8.3$ to $+3.1$, shape $p{=}0.61$) and $-5.9$
on \mQwen{} ($-12.8$ to $-0.4$, $p{=}0.077$; 5 tasks to 30). The
registered non-superiority predictions (49) and (49Q), that the governed
route does not lead by 5 points, both hold after Holm correction; neither
is an equivalence. The governed losses are not failures to fetch: every
wrong governed run on a task lost to the paste had looked up the fees
domain before its first fee query.

The plain wording lifts the governed contract by 4.5 points on \mLuna{}
and 4.7 on \mQwen{} (shape $p{=}0.059$; (50Q) fails), and the paste more:
on \mQwen{} the route's $+7.0$ in the original wording becomes $-5.9$
(interaction shape $p{=}0.016$; expl.). Placed in the contract, the facts
lift \mQwen{}'s governed arm less than as prompt notes beside it ($-3.7$
points; $-9.3$ over all 401, $p{=}0.012$).

\textbf{On \mQwen{}, the views split by which text is scored.} Of its
answers right end-to-end on the rule-matching tasks, the governed arm gives
the value alone, as the prompt asks, in 67\%, the text in 59\%. The
official scorer reads the whole message and rewards that compliance, so
under it the governed arm leads in 5d ($+5.4$, $p{=}0.024$) and 5e
($+7.5$): partly answer format, not a more often correct value (expl.).
\mLuna{} gives the value alone in every arm.

The other route contrasts agree: an authored contract on the
constructed questions (2 tasks to 0; Section~\ref{sec:gaps}), and compiled
knowledge on LiveSQLBench (25 tasks to 22 on \mLuna{}, 24 to 22 on
\mQwen{}), where the registered (3) had the tools ahead and failed, and an
authored contract on DataAgentBench ($+0.0$ and $-2.5$ points;
\appref{app:dab}).

\paragraph{Summary.} With the facts held equal and stated once, the
contract's form leads prose on the rule-matching tasks (unregistered). The
route does not deliver the deciding facts better than the paste, and on
LiveSQLBench makes no detectable difference. These contrasts vary by
model, task family and scoring view; none bounds what delivery can do.
