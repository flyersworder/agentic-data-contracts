\section{Where the Knowledge Comes From}
\label{sec:authoring}

If the contract's value is the knowledge it states, its value is its
author's. Section~\ref{sec:authoring-paths} describes three ways to author
one: interactively, a model drafting under the author's direction;
compiled, entry by entry, from the documentation; and written whole by a
model that reads all of the documentation and explores the data. This
section asks where the knowledge that did the work came from, on both
benchmarks. It is secondary to Sections~\ref{sec:knowledge}
and~\ref{sec:delivery}.

\subsection{Data inspection grounds encodings}
\label{sec:compiled}
\label{sec:better-compile}

The compiler reads a manual or KB entry by entry and turns each into a
contract definition, checking its SQL against the database read-only and
refusing what it cannot state. On DABStep a first compile (Sonnet~5)
scores 47.3\% on \mLuna{} over all tasks, against the original contract's
80.0\% in the same run. With Opus~5.5 as the compiler and a contract
format that can declare a multi-table metric, the fee formula compiles,
and the compiled contract reaches 76.6\% (172 tasks to 15 over the first
compile): $(76.6-47.3)/(80.0-47.3)=90\%$ of the way, for the compiler and
the joins together. Opus without joins scores 47.4\%. The stronger
compile's checks against the data found the empty-list wildcard of
Section~\ref{sec:one-fact}: its notes record that the data stores ``all''
as an empty list.

\textbf{Provenance of the second fact.} The same compile noted that
several fee rules can match one payment, and its fee definition returns one
row per matching pair, but it left open how their fees combine. The
original contract sums them, which is the gold's convention and also the
default join-and-sum idiom; neither the documentation nor the data settles
it. We do not credit the original contract's summing to data inspection.

Real documentation leaves things open. The gaps experiment opened six gaps
in the complete KBs of the constructed questions: a wildcard stored as a
sentinel (in all three databases), a quantity stored in an encoding the KB
does not state, and, in one database, the precedence among matching rules
removed. Blind model raters labelled each gap by what the data reveals:
two \emph{settled}, three \emph{split}, one \emph{convention-only}. An
author wrote three contracts per database in independent sessions with the
data open, and one without the data as a control.

Recovery was scored before any agent ran. The draws with data recovered
the two settled gaps in 6 of 6 draws, the three split gaps in 6 of 9, and
the convention gap in 0 of 3: 12 of 15 data-informed gap-draws. That
count credits the three reverse-logistics wildcard draws, which match all
4{,}493 record pairs but never compute specificity; scored partial,
recovery is 9 of 15 and registered (32) fails. The control without data
recovered none of five. Every session chose the lowest rule id for the
precedence gap, where the gold uses the most specific rule.

The agents then show the recovered knowledge doing the work
(Table~\ref{tab:gaps}). The authored contract beats the gapped KB by 26.8
points on the 41 data-informed questions, as registered in (35): 28 tasks
to 1, but from five gaps, of which four favour it and one ties (one-sided
$p{=}0.06$; five clusters allow no less than 0.03). By model it is $+50$
on \mLuna{}, $+26$ on \mSol{} and $+4$ on \mQwen{} (not significant). The
share of that gain the author's prose notes reproduce is 1.03 (0.98 to
1.35, over only five gaps). \textbf{What the author recovered, and what
the author got wrong, travelled through prose notes and through the
contract alike}: on the encoding the data only suggested and on the
precedence convention, every arm built from that author scores 0, and the
complete KB 97 to 98\%.

\subsection{LiveSQLBench: knowledge helps, and the KB often leaves the
gold open}
\label{sec:lsb-knowledge}

LiveSQLBench supplies each database's knowledge base (KB), so its
knowledge arms are built from the benchmark's own documentation
(Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks}). On the 302 primary tasks the KB as a
manual beats the bare schema on both models, as registered in (1):
$+15.6$ points on \mLuna{} (35.5\% against 20.0\%; over databases $+10.6$
to $+20.6$) and $+14.1$ on \mQwen{} (37.3\% against 23.2\%; $+9.1$ to
$+19.0$). Compiled knowledge does what the manual does: \mLuna{} scores
36.8\% with the manual plus every compiled metric's SQL and 35.9\% with
the compiled contract behind the governed tools, and \mQwen{} 37.7\% and
37.7\% (Section~\ref{sec:tools-add}). The agent reads what it is given,
looking a metric up in 86 to 93\% of compiled-contract runs.

Observed accuracy is low. Two gold-aware model raters (Section~\ref{sec:threats}),
each blind to every run and contract, read each task's question, gold SQL
and result and the KB entries the gold uses, and judged whether the
question and the KB settle every choice that changes the result. They
agree on 83\% of tasks ($\kappa{=}0.79$). Both find the KB leaves a
result-changing choice open on 132 of the 302 tasks; across the four rater
pairings of two families the count ranges from 112 to 146. No human checked
the labels. The knowledge arms solve about 60\% of the tasks the KB
settles and 14\% of those it leaves open (53 to 62\% and 8 to 14\% under
the other rater families), but the bare schema differs too (39\% against
6\%). The knowledge's own gain is larger where the KB settles the gold in
points (expl.), $+20.8$ against $+7.6$ (difference $+13.2$, over databases $+4.8$ to
$+22.4$), and equal in log-odds ($+0.84$ against $+0.89$): from baselines
of 39\% and 6\%, a larger effect of knowledge cannot be separated from the
floor. The open
choices were most often how a \texttt{NULL} or an unlisted category is
scored, which fields a formula averages, and which of two columns holds a
quantity.

\textbf{Better authors and oracle corrections.} On 55 tasks of three
databases, on \mLuna{}, neither a stronger compile with declared joins nor
an author session that read the whole KB and explored the data showed a
detectable gain over the first compile: $+1.8$ points ($-3.0$ to $+7.3$)
and $-1.8$ ($-9.1$ to $+5.5$). Corrections written from the golds of half
of each database's tasks, as general facts about KB entries, lift those
tasks by about 21 points; on the held-out half no comparable transfer was
observed (the manual $+3.6$ points, interval 0 to $+9.5$, on 28 tasks of
three databases).
Each database's primary tasks were split in half before anything was
written. From the gold of the 27 training tasks only, 35 corrections were
written as general facts about KB entries (a scale, a threshold, how a
missing value counts, a grain), never a question's filter or output form,
and applied both to the manual and to the Opus contract. They lift the
training tasks by 7 tasks to 0 in each delivery. On the 28 held-out tasks
they lift the manual by 2 tasks to 0 ($+3.6$ points, interval 0 to
$+9.5$) and the contract not at all (0 tasks to 2), although half of those
tasks use an entry a correction names; two training golds already used one
entry differently. The oracle rows are not released.

\subsection{Complete prose carries a procedure}
\label{sec:procedures}
\label{sec:account}

Where the knowledge is complete, prose suffices. The constructed questions
state a rule-matching procedure and a composite of dependent formulas for
each of three databases completely; every knowledge arm, prose or contract,
plain or in a manual's style, scores 95.6 to 100\% on \mLuna{}, \mSol{} and
\mQwen{}. That ceiling means these questions cannot rank representations or
procedure types.
