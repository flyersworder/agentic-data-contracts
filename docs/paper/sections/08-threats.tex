\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\subsection{One benchmark, one domain}

Section~\ref{sec:domain} showed that the gain is confined to fee questions,
which are 294 of the 332 hard tasks. The benchmark is also a single and
concentrated one: 450 tasks reduce to 113 question shapes, 27 of which cover
three quarters of the benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:dabstep}). No amount of
compute fixes that, and results on so narrow a question space should not be
read as results about analytics agents in general. We ran no second benchmark. BIRD's withheld evidence~\cite{bird} is the
nearest analogue, but it supplies a hint per question rather than a domain
manual, and a domain manual is what a contract is compiled from.

\subsection{The contract resolves ambiguities the manual leaves open}
\label{sec:interpretation}

The arms are matched on the sources they may consult, not on how those
sources read. Seven metric descriptions carry an \texttt{INTERPRETATION}
sentence (Section~\ref{sec:contract}). Three gloss wording the manual
supplies outright; one derives the capture-delay band mapping by applying
the manual's own band definitions to a column of days; three go further. The
manual says a \texttt{null} field ``applies to all possible values'', but the
annexed \texttt{fees} data encodes that wildcard for list-typed fields as an
\emph{empty list}, which the manual never mentions, and two band metrics
break a tie on values sitting exactly on a shared boundary, which the
manual's ``between A and B'' does not decide. All three are recoverable from
the annexed data, which every arm could query, and none supplies a task's
answer. But they were recovered once, by us, and handed to one arm, and the
empty-list clause matters: reversing it in the compiled contract changes the
answer on 87 of the 97 tasks in the payments families. The contract arm's
margin therefore combines delivery of what the manual states with resolution
of what it leaves open. Separating them needs a fifth arm carrying the
contract with its seven clauses stripped, which we did not run.

\subsection{No repeats, and two frontier points that disagree}
\label{sec:power}

None of the four runs repeats, so none estimates within-condition variance.
One near-replicate exists: the all-450 \armC{} sweep on \mGLM{} submitted to
the leaderboard (Section~\ref{sec:golds}) shares 401 tasks with the \mGLM{}
run and differs from it only by a validator fix. Paired, the two agree on
accuracy (60.3\% against 57.9\%, exact McNemar $p{=}0.35$) and
\textbf{disagree on 94 of 401 tasks, a 23.4\% flip rate}, at temperature 0
on a pinned endpoint. The churn is symmetric (52 one way, 42 the other), so
the McNemar contrasts, which read asymmetry, are not at risk; but noise
alone supplies 94 discordant pairs against the 86 to 182 our contrasts rest
on, so no claim about a named task's verdict should be read as
reproducible, and the frontier runs, which could not pin temperature, are
likelier to flip more. Table~\ref{tab:mcnemar} reports 24 tests and
corrects none; the three vs-\armC{} contrasts survive any correction, and
the scaffolding row on the two flash models does not. Underpowered
comparisons are the norm in this literature~\cite{little-power}.

The capability claims rest on two frontier models that disagree, by 21
points on the \emph{macro} bucket and by 4, the other way, on
\emph{derived}. Capability is also not one number here: \mSON{} ties \mDS{}
on the bare-schema arm we use as the axis and sits 12 points above it with
the contract, so the two orderings agree on rank and not on spacing. The
literature does not settle the direction either~\cite{prompt-engineering-advanced,scaffold-gaia},
and the frontier runs differ from the flash runs in provider and model
family as well as capability.

One lesson from how this paper was written belongs here. With only the two
flash models, the scaffolding step was a null with disagreeing signs and a
pooled $p{=}0.29$; the two frontier models put it at $+15$ and $+14$ points
at $p{<}10^{-6}$. The same two models also showed a margin over the prompt
that shrank with capability and a derivation gap fixed near 38 points, and
neither survived a third. \textbf{A null across two adjacent models is weak
evidence of absence}, and the size of the derivation gap, which rests on two
frontier points 21 apart, deserves the same discount.

\subsection{Reconstructed golds are lenient}
\label{sec:threats-golds}

Every accuracy in this paper except the officially graded pair rests on
reconstructed golds that are one-sidedly lenient on the hard split
(Section~\ref{sec:golds}); read every absolute hard-split figure as a few
points optimistic. The proxy that bounds the other cells shows \armC{} to be
the least exposed arm on all four models but the one with the most correct
answers to lose, so the correction moves the twelve \armC{}-versus-baseline
gaps by $-2.8$ to $+1.4$ points against raw gaps of 13.7 to 45.5
(\appref{app:leniency}). No conclusion here turns on 3 points.

\subsection{Contamination}

DABStep is public, and so is the material an agent needs to answer it. We
ran no contamination probe. This bears on the capability claims, because
the four models differ in training data as well as capability, and a
memorising model would show exactly what we report: high accuracy on the
\emph{macro} bucket, whose tasks follow the benchmark's most common shapes,
and less on \emph{derived}. We do not believe that explains the result,
because \mGPT{}'s contract arm scores 94.9\% against 31.8\% for its
bare-schema arm on the same tasks, and \mSON{}'s 73.9\% against 6.8\%, and
memorisation should not care which context the agent was handed. That is an
inference, not a measurement.

\subsection{Harness asymmetries}

Section~\ref{sec:fixed} listed them: a validator defect on \mGLM{} against
the governed arms, a preview cap in their favour, a forced-answer policy
against them, and different provider routes across models. \appref{app:harness}
adds two more: a 1{,}000-row result limit, since replaced, under which the
governed arm looked $11{\times}$ more expensive, and a digest stamp that
pinned the hollow arm's rows to the wrong artifact without changing any
result. Each run's internal contrasts are sound; cross-run cost
differences are suggestive only.
