\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Context layers work. The open question was which part of one does the work,
and it matters because the parts cost different amounts: semantic prose is
written once per domain and often exists already, retrieval scaffolding is
engineering paid once, and pre-computed macros are paid per metric forever.

Holding the tool surface, the retrieval instruction, the table allow-list
and the operation rules byte-for-byte fixed and emptying only the prose,
\textbf{the content step dominates the scaffolding step on every model}: by
$6.6{\times}$ on the weakest model, entirely on the one where scaffolding
does nothing at all, and by $2.0{\times}$ and $1.8{\times}$ on the two
frontier models.
The contract beats the same knowledge in a prompt because its rule is
reachable at the moment of use, visibly so in the SQL it submits, where it
writes all six fee clauses up to 98\% of the time against the prompt arm's
4\%; on one model the prompt is indistinguishable from a bare schema on hard
tasks while the contract is 45 points above both. It does so with the fewest
turns and the least reasoning on every model, and at the lowest cost per
correct answer on three of four. On the fourth, a ten-to-one price ratio
between fresh and cached input tokens charges on-demand delivery more than
it charges a cached prompt.

Because the contract's declared expressions compile to a layer that
reproduces gold exactly, each arm's accuracy is a recovery rate against a
proven ceiling. The contract arm recovers 60.8\%, 62.7\%, 73.9\% and 94.9\%
of what it was handed, in order of capability: \textbf{the cost of requiring
an agent to derive, rather than handing it a pre-computed macro, is
transient}, and already small on the strongest model we ran. The composition
gap above it does not track capability.

The same transcripts carry a narrower result. Declared rules that the model
merely reads stopped every mutating statement it would otherwise have
submitted, 166 to zero on the three models that attempted a write, one of
them frontier-class, while the enforcement layer behind those rules never
had to fire.

Three limits bound all of this. The gain appears only inside the domain the
contract describes. The benchmark is a single and concentrated one. And none
of the four runs repeats: the one near-replicate flips 23.4\% of per-task
verdicts at temperature 0, which leaves the aggregate comparisons standing
and no individual verdict reproducible.

\paragraph{Future work,} in order of what it would settle. Repeat runs on
the two models whose temperature can be pinned would put a variance estimate
under every number here. A fifth arm, the contract with its seven
interpretation clauses stripped, would separate delivery of what the manual
states from resolution of what it leaves open. A fifth model would say
whether the derivation gap's size is a function of anything the bare-schema
arm measures. And an executable metric layer, a tool that runs a declared
expression rather than describing it, would separate the declarative
contribution from the executable one the way this paper separates content
from scaffolding. It was deliberately not built before this measurement,
because it would be designed from knowledge of which tasks the contract arm
lost, and that would spend the strongest methodological asset here: an
artifact frozen before any question was read. MotherDuck's later experiment,
in which a generated Malloy semantic layer scored 95\% against 100\% for
Markdown plus SQL while costing $2.5{\times}$ the
tokens~\cite{motherduck-ai-writes}, is a with/without comparison on that
component, and it lands where our ablation does. The order is measure, ship, re-measure; this paper is the baseline.
