\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{Two benchmarks, one natural procedure.} The contract's form gain
is measured on tasks governed by a rule-matching procedure, and the only
natural one in either benchmark is DABStep's fee matching. The constructed questions add three more,
but they are ours, and complete prose carries them at the ceiling. A second, independent benchmark whose documentation states
rules once for many tasks would test the form result properly; EntSQL,
DataSpace and BI-Bench give nearly every task its own document or project,
leaving no rules to state once~\cite{entsql,dataspace,bi-bench}.

\paragraph{The raters are models.} The gap raters were \mSol{} and
Opus~5.5; the raters of determinacy and procedural load, the failure
auditors and the checks of the constructed questions were separate
Opus~5.5 sessions. The determinacy raters read the gold, so
``determined'' is partly ``a reader of the KB would reach this gold''
(Section~\ref{sec:lsb-knowledge}); their plan was written before any
rating was read but committed only with the results, the procedural-load
plan before. One rater each from two other families, GPT-6.1~sol and
Gemini~4~Argon, sandboxed, re-rated every item under a plan committed
first. Their agreement with the Opus raters matches the Opus raters' own
on DABStep ($\kappa$ 0.84 to 0.97) and is lower on determinacy (0.64 to
0.75, against 0.79); every significant subset result holds on their
subsets but one on Gemini's, which keeps its sign
(\appref{app:cross-family}). No human has checked any rater.

\ifextended
\paragraph{Ceilings and the scale regime.} The constructed questions sit at
the ceiling at their base level, so they could show a drop but not a gain,
and they are easier than the benchmarks' own. The scale experiment's
questions name their metric, and its padding is other databases under
distinct names: the regime in which long prompts are known to hold up
(Section~\ref{sec:scale}).
\fi

\paragraph{Selection and adaptive reuse.} The second fact was found by
reading 34 tasks' transcripts, and \mLuna{}'s registered test of it reuses
the runs that picked those tasks. \mQwen{}'s registered test, on
transcripts nobody read, and \mLuna{}'s third repeat alone carry the claim
without that reuse. The 165 tasks not read are held out by task, not by
question shape (Section~\ref{sec:second-fact}). The rule-matching subset was chosen after a
load prediction failed, so effects measured on it are enriched for the fee
procedure and do not describe DABStep as a whole. More broadly, the 401
tasks were reused across the series: each experiment was designed from
what the one before found, and the notes were written from transcripts of
this test set. A prediction fixes its test before its run, not the
sequence of questions asked, so the series is adaptive in the sense
of~\cite{ladder}, and its later contrasts are conditional on the earlier
findings.

\paragraph{Sampling and harness.} Temperature is 0 for \mGLM{}, \mDS{}
and \mQwen{} and the provider's default for the GPT models and \mSON{},
whose routes reject it. In \mGLM{}'s
single run the validator wrongly rejected \texttt{COUNT(*)} 79 times, a
defect fixed before the later runs that handicapped only that run's
governed arms; and governed previews returned up to 100 rows where the
plain tools return 50, on 31 calls, in the governed arms' favour.

\paragraph{Provenance of the original contract.} The golds existed an
hour before its first freeze, in the same working tree
(\appref{app:golds}); we can show the rule against consulting them, not
that it was kept. The Opus compile, made without the golds, surfaced the
wildcard and the overlap of rules from the data
(Section~\ref{sec:better-compile}), a partial check.

\ifextended
\paragraph{Scoring views and graders.}\label{sec:threats-golds} \mQwen{} states its working before
its answer, which the official DABStep scorer marks wrong; we report the
end-to-end view beside it, and in the second-fact run the order of the arms
changes between the two views. The vendored DABStep scorer also ignores the
sign of a number: on nine fee-change questions with negative golds, 114
official verdicts and 43 end-to-end ones credit an answer of the wrong
sign, mostly in \mQwen{}'s baseline arms. Re-grading with the sign enforced
changes no prediction's outcome; over all tasks it moves \mQwen{}'s
scaffolding step from $-4.3$ to $-3.4$ points (official) and from $+0.2$ to
$+1.2$ (end-to-end), and we keep the official verdicts. LiveSQLBench's official
rules strip every \texttt{DISTINCT} from both answer and gold, which leans
against the compiled contracts' \texttt{IS DISTINCT FROM}; grading without
the stripping moves each arm by under two points and no outcome. DABStep's
reconstructed golds run 3 to 6 points high on the hard split, on both
arms of the officially graded pair.
\else
\paragraph{Scoring views and graders.}\label{sec:threats-golds} The
official DABStep scorer marks \mQwen{}'s answer wrong when it follows its
working, hence the end-to-end view, and it ignores a number's sign;
enforcing the sign, or not stripping \texttt{DISTINCT} on LiveSQLBench,
changes no prediction's outcome. DABStep's reconstructed golds run 3 to 6
points high on both arms of the officially graded pair.

\paragraph{Dependence, variance and multiplicity.}\label{sec:power} Tasks
cluster, and effects are reported with intervals over clusters
(Section~\ref{sec:prereg}). The knowledge step, \mSol{}'s scaffolding
deficit, the facts' lift and \mLuna{}'s form gain survive clustering by
question shape; the gaps experiment's registered gain is four gaps of
five with one tied (one-sided $p{=}0.06$); \mQwen{}'s form gain, \mLuna{}'s
tool gain on the fee-average families, the one-fact shares' ordering and
the Qwen scaffolding step's sign are not established over shapes.
A near-replicate of \mGLM{}'s contract arm disagreed with the original on
94 of 401 tasks, so the four single runs are read only through paired
tests. Apart from Holm within experiment~5e, no multiplicity correction is
applied across the 63 predictions. At the task level, the unit of their
registered tests, every registered directional prediction that held
survives a Bonferroni correction over the 57 fixed before 5e except (19),
marginally, and (35) at the cluster level; 5e's (53) survives over all 63. At the shape level, with 5e's engine applied afterwards (expl.), (28Q)
survives over the 165 unread tasks ($p{=}3{\times}10^{-5}$) but not over the
105 in unread shapes ($p{=}0.004$), nor does (27) ($p{=}0.02$); the eight
registered ties read one equivalent within $\pm$5 points, one different
and six inconclusive: a tie that held did so by not rejecting. Exploratory
$p$-values between 0.001 and 0.05 would not survive a correction and are
hypotheses.

\paragraph{Scope.} One harness, the authors', runs every arm, and both
benchmarks' schemas fit in a context window; where they do not, retrieval
is needed and the delivery result may not
transfer~\cite{death-schema-linking,spider2}. No arm retrieves from the
original documentation. The two-fact attribution and the delivery
experiments ran on \mLuna{} and \mQwen{} only; \mSol{} was given only the
first fact. Every arm writes SQL, so what an executable semantic layer's
pre-computed metrics add is not measured. Each delivery contrast is read within one co-run block
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder}), and comparisons across blocks are not paired.

\paragraph{Our interest.} We wrote the library we evaluate, and report
where it does not help: its tools without knowledge cost \mSol{}
accuracy, a first compile is worth the manual, and at one database the
prompt is cheaper. Both benchmarks are public, and we did not measure
contamination.
