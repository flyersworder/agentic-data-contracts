\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Context layers are evaluated with and without, which credits their
machinery with whatever knowledge they add. Holding one fixed while
varying the other, we find on DABStep that emptying a contract's knowledge
while keeping its tools costs 26 to 50 hard-task points on six models,
and that its lead over the pasted manual is mostly two pieces of data
semantics the manual omits: a wildcard stored as an empty list, and fees
that add up when several rules match. Stated plainly in prose, they
recover most of that lead on \mLuna{} and, end-to-end, all of it on
\mQwen{}; how plainly they are stated matters as much as whether they
are. With the facts held equal, the contract's form leads prose
provisionally on the rule-matching tasks, and its route shows no
consistent accuracy difference once both arms state the semantics.
Before crediting a context layer's machinery, check that the comparison
holds its semantics equal.

Delivery is then a choice on other grounds. Against a verbose YAML in the
prompt, looking the contract up is cheaper from twice a database's size on
the GPT models, and works where the YAML no longer fits. A prose KB of the
same knowledge fits too and, on \mQwen{}, lost no accuracy at 22
databases' worth, at about seven times the input tokens. The governed route leaves a log of the
definitions each answer consulted. No run whose prompt stated the
forbidden operations corrupted its database copy, against 50 runs without
them, nearly all on one model; that is an association, not a test of
enforcement.

\paragraph{Future work.} The knowledge ladder run together on two models,

scripts, the blind ratings, the run commits and the raw result rows are
tracked at \url{https://github.com/flyersworder/agentic-data-contracts}

ones are archived as \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23598749}; 208 of the later
runs' 57{,}834 rows have none, because a pod restart lost the transcripts
written before it, and the release lists them. LiveSQLBench's
private gold, test cases and task mapping are not redistributed, nor are
its transcripts, since one graded correct is a solution the benchmark
keeps private; its rows carry a hash of the gold result, and the analyses
that use the gold print aggregates only.
