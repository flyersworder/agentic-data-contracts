\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Context layers work; the open question was which part of one does the
work. On two benchmarks and seven models the answer is the knowledge.
Emptying a contract's prose while keeping its tools and rules removes most
of its gain on every model; the tools without the prose are worth between
$-9$ and $+15$ points, far less than the content. What looked like an advantage of delivery on DABStep was
mostly two facts the manual left out; stated alike, the contract's format
adds a few points at most, and its tools add a rule put in view as the
query is written, on some task families. A contract is worth what its author could determine: compiled
from the documentation it is worth the documentation, written by a careful
author it carries a determined procedure exactly, and on LiveSQLBench,
where the gold settles what the documentation leaves open, no author
gains. An author with the data recovers what the data settles and nothing
the data cannot reveal.

Delivery still matters, for other reasons. Looking a contract up as
needed costs no accuracy; its cost rises by about a third as the contract
grows to 22 databases, while the prompt's rises elevenfold; and it runs
where the prompt no longer fits. The lookup log names the definition behind each answer,
which makes a wrong answer traceable to the entry that caused it. And the
contract's declared rules kept every governed run from writing to the
database, across every DABStep run we made.

\paragraph{Future work,} in order of what it would settle. A second
benchmark whose documentation states rules once for many tasks would test
whether the contract's form gain is general or belongs to one procedure. A
contract padded with neighbouring domains of one warehouse, rather than
other databases, is the harder test of a long prompt. Human raters would
check the model raters on which the determinacy and load results rest. And
a validator that can state ``a filter on this list column must accept the
empty-list wildcard'' would let the governed tools catch the error they now
only prevent by retrieval.

\section*{Artifact Availability}

The library, the harness, every contract and compiler, the analysis
scripts, the run commits and the raw result rows are tracked at
\url{https://github.com/flyersworder/agentic-data-contracts}. Every row
records its commit, contract digest, gold-set hash, scorer, model,
endpoint, token counts, cost and a post-run integrity check of its
database copy. The transcripts of the four single runs are attached to
the release tagged \texttt{dabstep-eval-2026-09}, with SHA-256 digests.
LiveSQLBench's gold SQL, gold results and task-to-knowledge mapping are
the benchmark's private material and are not redistributed; analyses that
depend on them print aggregates only.
