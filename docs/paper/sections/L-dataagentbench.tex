\section{A Second Benchmark: DataAgentBench}
\label{app:dab}

DataAgentBench~\cite{dab-berkeley} asks natural-language questions over
datasets spread across several database systems. For each dataset its
builders supply a description and a file of hints that hold for many
queries, the property Section~\ref{sec:threats} asks for. We used the 40
leaderboard queries of the nine datasets that need no MongoDB, loaded each
dataset into one DuckDB session, and graded every answer with the query's
own validator. We asked the maintainers whether these adaptations, and
publishing material derived from the data, are acceptable; they had not
answered at the time of writing.

\textbf{Contracts.} An Opus~5.5 author session per dataset had the
description, the schema, sample rows and read-only SQL on the data, and
neither the questions nor the hints (three draws, \emph{A}); one more had
the hints too (\emph{B}); a control had no data. Before any agent ran, a
checker that never saw the questions scored, for each hint item, whether a
draw's contract recovered it. Draw~A recovered 12 of 36 data-discoverable
item-draws, against a registered half ((43) fails), and 31 of 57 items of
the other classes ((43w), which predicted fewer, fails). The control without
data recovered 1 of 12 ((43c) holds), so recovery came from the data, not
from memory of the benchmark.

\textbf{Agent runs.} Five arms ran together, $k{=}5$, on \mLuna{} and
\mQwen{}: the description alone; with the hints; with draw~A1's contract
behind the governed tools; with its files in the prompt over plain tools;
and with the hints and draw~B's governed contract.

The registered manipulation check (44), that the hints beat the
description, fails on both models: $-0.5$ points on \mLuna{} (10 queries
to 9) and $+8.0$ on \mQwen{} (12 to 6, $p{=}0.12$, in the hints' favour on
7 of the 8 datasets where the arms differ). By the reading fixed before
the run, the four predictions that rest on it are reported and not read as
support. The contract alone does not beat the description ((45): $+0.0$
and $+8.0$). The route makes no detectable difference ((47) holds on both:
$+0.0$ and $-2.5$ points). The hints with draw~B's contract score highest
on both models, $+10.5$ and $+7.0$ points over the hints ((48): $p{=}0.032$
on \mLuna{}, failing whenever one of four datasets is left out, and
$p{=}0.40$ on \mQwen{}).

\textbf{Leniency.} A validator accepts an answer that contains the gold,
however long. Such passes, more than 200 characters longer than the gold,
are 6.5 to 11.5\% of \mLuna{}'s runs per arm and 30.5 to 41.5\% of
\mQwen{}'s, which writes out its working. Read without them, as registered
where arms differ by more than 5 points in them, \mQwen{}'s route contrast
becomes $-12.4$ points (3 queries to 6) and (47) fails.

With 2 to 13 queries per dataset, one dataset moves every test, and the
benchmark's own hints did not detectably help either model. The test of
whether a contract does the hints' work therefore could not be read; the
route result is the one this benchmark repeats.
