Review-schedule wildcards: The documentation says a NULL criterion is a wildcard; the contract finds that no criterion is stored as SQL NULL and that the wildcard is the text '*' (222 of 496 rules have visa_class '*'). Treat either NULL or '*' as “no restriction”; '*' does not count toward rule specificity. With this interpretation, 69 of 981 cases use the default schedule, and the contract reports 4,188.00 total Case Review Hours across all cases.

Dates: recvDay is text in YYYY/MM/DD format (every receipt is 2023/12/21), decisionDay is text in YYYY-MM-DD format (NULL for 216 cases), and beginDay is text in DD/MM/YYYY format (always present, spanning December 2023 to December 2024). Parse each according to its own format, never compare the date text lexically. endDay is free text such as '2026 20th Dec.' and is not parsed by any metric; origcertday is always NULL. APT is NULL without a decision date.

Statuses and certification: Although the documented outcomes include Denied, Withdrawn and Certified-Withdrawn, this data stores only 'CERTIFIED', 'Certified' and 'certified'; every one of the 981 cases is certified. Test certified after trimming and ignoring case; Certified-Withdrawn would not count. Approval and success rates on this data are 100%.

Employer and industry identity: The employer table's key is (corphandle, zipref); a case joins on both its homefirm and homezip, and all 981 cases join. For employer-level metrics use the exact text of cases.homeFirm, without case folding or trimming: there are 546 distinct names, versus 540 when case-folded. NAICS is read from employer_contact_info's naics_code; some stored codes have fewer than the documented six digits (for example, 54151), so use them as stored. Industry means this NAICS code.

Attorney representation and identity: A case is represented when it has a case_attorney row, not merely when counsel information is otherwise present; 727 of 981 cases have exactly one such row, counselFor is always 'Yes', and a case without a row is self-represented. Match case_attorney.counselMail to attorney.lawMail. ECR counts distinct represented cases against all cases; ACL and attorney approval rates count distinct case identifiers for each counselMail.

Wage storage and annual comparisons: Each case has exactly one case_worksite row, linked by dockKey to fileKey, and its wageTrack links to prevailing_wage.trackNo without orphans. Offered and prevailing amounts and their separate payment units are inside wage_details: use offered_wage.from, ignoring 'to', remove '$' and commas; use prevailing_wage.value, removing 'USD ' and commas. The contract finds all offered 'from' values parse, all prevailing values start with 'USD ' and parse, and offered units seen are Hour, Week and Year (prevailing units include Month). WDR and WPR compare the resulting annualized amounts, using each object's own unit factor; WDR is a percentage, or 100 times the fractional WHR.

Wage Premium Rate: “the data holds only the prevailing wage, so it is used as the minimum (WPR then equals WDR), on annualized amounts.”

Review volume when filtering: For monthly_volume_band and governing rules, compute employer monthly case volume over all cases before applying any outer filter; a filtered report does not recalculate the band from its subset.

Application Complexity Score and risk: Because the documented ACS weights are not stated, complexity_factor_count is an unweighted count, adding one each for h1bDep = 'Yes', willfulV = 'Yes', newemp greater than zero and amendflag greater than zero. Delay risk uses these factors: willfulV = 'Yes' gives High Risk; otherwise all three of dependency, new employment and amendment give High Risk, one or two give Moderate Risk, and none give Low Risk. The data holds only 'No' or NULL for willfulV.

Application Complexity Tiers: “'special attestations' has no column, so Complex = h1bdep = 'Yes' OR willfulv = 'Yes'”; otherwise the contract assigns Standard. H-1B dependency is stored as a Yes/No/NULL flag and is used rather than recomputed, because workforce size is unavailable.

Visa Filing Window: The contract interprets months as calendar-month differences between receipt and employment start, rather than elapsed months. A start before the receipt date is 'After Start Date'; otherwise a difference of zero is 'Same Month', one to three '1-3 Months Before', four to six 'Optimal Window (4-6 Months)', and over six 'Early Filing'. The data has 321 Optimal, 393 one-to-three-month, 247 Same Month and 20 Early cases.

Attorney Performance Rating: The contract computes the rating from attorney_success_rate using distinct cases per attorney and the trimmed, case-insensitive certified test. Over 95% is 'High Performer'; 85% through 95% inclusive is 'Standard Performer' (exactly 85% and exactly 95% are Standard); below 85% is 'Underperformer'.

Legal Representation Efficacy: The gap is the approval rate with attorney minus the approval rate without, in percentage points, not a relative percent change. The contract labels a gap over 10 points High Impact, 5 through 10 Moderate Impact, and an absolute gap under 5 Minimal Impact; a gap of −5 or lower is 'Unclassified'. Both groups have 100% approval here, yielding a zero gap and Minimal Impact.

Attorney specialization: ASI's total number of visa types is fixed at four—all four documented classes occur in this data—and an attorney's handled types are distinct classes. attorney_top_visa_class_share is the fraction of an attorney's distinct cases in that attorney's most frequent visa class; a share over 0.80 qualifies as Specialist. No threshold is specified for Generalist or Hybrid Practitioner, so the contract does not assign those categories quantitatively.

Worksite counts: State of an application is case_worksite.wsState from its single worksite row. Worksite Density divides the sum of case_worksite.wsHeads by the number of distinct worksite addresses, identified by address line 1, city, state and ZIP, in scope. Worksite Diversity Index groups the same address fields by exact employer name, sums wsHeads at each address, and takes each address's share of that employer's total workers. The worksite master also records SecEnt as a secondary-entity flag (Yes/No, NULL unspecified).

Employer retention and annual size: A continuation application for ERR is a case with contemp (continued-employment positions) greater than zero. Employer Size Classification counts applications per exact employer name and receipt year, not start year; all receipts here are in 2023. A Continuous Filing Employer needs receipt months in at least nine months of a receipt year; all receipts fall on December 21, 2023, so none qualifies.

Wage Competitiveness Index: Both employer and industry averages use annualized offered wages per case. Industry is the stored employer NAICS code, and its average includes all cases with that NAICS; compare that with the average of the employer's cases in that industry.

Wage Growth Rate: The contract takes “similar positions” to mean the same SOC code, “year” to mean employment-start year from beginDay, and wage to mean annualized offered wage. It compares average wages for a SOC code in consecutive start years, and returns NULL when the immediately preceding year has no cases; both 2023 and 2024 starts occur.

Skill Shortage Occupation: For the WDR portion, the contract interprets occupation WDR as average case WDR by SOC code, tests whether that average exceeds 10%, and calls for reporting the application count too. It does not determine statistical significance because the significance test is not stated; the WDR-only result is not the complete shortage designation.

Receipt-based and result-based averages: Seasonal Application Index groups by calendar month of receipt and averages over months present in the grouped result, not empty months; every receipt is in one month, making SAI 1. ODI averages application counts over SOC codes present in its grouped result; ESI averages them over employers present. VCD and EHC use totals over their grouped results, with EHC's denominator all H-1B applications in that result. PER uses the average processing time among result rows with decision dates, and is NULL if a case's APT is NULL or zero.

Industry and geography denominators: IAD and ICR group applications by the employer's stored NAICS code; SAD and GCI group by case_worksite.wsState. SAD and GCI count distinct case identifiers, so an application is counted once in its worksite state.

Sources and other stored flags: Prevailing-wage level and source details are fields inside prevailing_wage.wage_details: prevailing_wage.level may be JSON null; OES uses prevailing_wage.oes_year, while alternate_source contains source_type (CBA or Survey), source_year, publisher and survey_title. cases.FullTimeInd holds 'Y', 'N' or NULL. cases.pubDisc also contains 'Disclose Employment' and 'Disclose Business and Employment' besides 'Disclose Business'. The case visa-class counts are H-1B 955, E-3 Australian 19, H-1B1 Singapore 5 and H-1B1 Chile 2.

Definitions the contract does not calculate from this data: CLAW and WkCI lack cost-of-living data; PPR lacks a premium-processing column; Visa-Dependent Industry and Employer Dependency Level lack workforce data; Geographic Application Hotspot lacks population data. The contract says to answer qualitatively or say the input is lacking rather than compute these classifications.

Unspecified classification rules: Occupational Specialization Levels and Position Scarcity Index have no assignment rule. Seasonal Application Pattern has no Peak/Off-Peak/Transition thresholds; use SAI as evidence, noting all receipts are on one date. Worksite Geographic Diversity has no defined regions: only Single-Location, interpreted as one distinct worksite, is determinable.
